*Public works takings caused almost a quarter of post-confiscation Māori land loss in Tauranga Moana.* 

Belgrave, Michael; Young, Grant; Heinz, Adam; Belgrave, David, [‘Tauranga Māori Land Alienation: A Quantitative Overview, 1886-2006’](https://forms.justice.govt.nz/search/Documents/WT/wt_DOC_93401142/Wai%20215%2C%20T016%20(a).pdf), research report commissioned by the Waitangi Tribunal, 2006, 131pp (Wai 215 T16a).  

---

The most substantial and the largest number of public works takings of Māori land in Tauranga Moana occurred after 1940 and particularly as a consequence of urban expansion as Tauranga built the infrastructure required for a city.  

The Māori land in this study was reserved for Māori following the Katikati Te Puna purchase, or was returned to Māori after being confiscated under the New Zealand Settlements Act.  

Adam Heinz directed a team of research assistants to search the Lexis-Nexis ‘New Zealand Gazette Archive’ to provide a further source of public works takings data to the quantitative database. The New Zealand Gazette is the single most significant and complete source of data on public works takings. Gazetted proclamations of public works takings almost always record the following information:  

- The name of the land parcel
- The survey block and survey district in which that block can be found
- The purpose for which the land is being taken
- The legislative authority for the taking
- The amount of land being taken
- The date the taking takes effect  

This confirmed that the existing research had already identified all the major public works takings in Tauranga Moana and that the remainder were largely road takings.

With over 3% of the total area of land returned or reserved taken by Public Works, the major issues are related to the location of specific takings and the processes involved in taking Public Works land.  

What is clearly evident from these figures is that Māori land, located as it was around the settlements of Tauranga and Mount Maunganui was very significantly affected by the expansion of those settlements.
