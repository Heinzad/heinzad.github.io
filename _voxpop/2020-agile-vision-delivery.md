*Balancing Alignment and Autonomy*  

How does an organisation work with multiple agile teams? Microsoft’s answer was to [make the whole organisation agile](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stevedenning/2015/10/27/surprise-microsoft-is-agile/#1f9163b22867).  They term this “Agile at Scale”. The key driver is developing [an organisational culture that balances alignment with autonomy](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stevedenning/2015/10/29/microsofts-sixteen-keys-to-becoming-agile-at-scale/#3a927f5815ce): “To scale Agile, [you must enable autonomy for the team, while ensuring alignment with the organisation](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/learn/agile/scale-agile-large-teams)”.

A key driver has been cultural change following the increasing understanding that software development is a creative activity, part of the “new industrial revolution” that requires a new approach to organisational management. 

A key principle is to balance alignment with autonomy:

• Alignment: Happens from the top down, ensures people understand what we're going to do and that people are lined up on business goals, aspects, and why we're trying to do what we're doing

• Autonomy: Happens from the bottom up, ensures people come into work every day and feel like they have a big impact on day-to-day activities and decisions

Microsoft’s organisational changes have been driven by a change in culture.  Vertical, cross-discipline, self-managing teams of 10-12 people are self-selecting: [every 18 months people can choose what team they want to be in](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/learn/devops-at-microsoft/agile-principles-in-practice#self-forming-teams).

Vertical teams are focused on end-to-end delivery of one aspect of the product. The teams are transparent and accountable: at the end of each sprint they send an email (or video) of the product they developed in that sprint, and what they will be developing in the next sprint. They have a quality control measure of 5 bugs per developer per sprint: if this cap is exceeded, new development is frozen until the team is under cap.

Microsoft identified a [line of autonomy](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/learn/agile/scale-agile-large-teams#line-of-autonomy): for example, management aligns organisational vision with the epics and features they own, while teams have autonomy on how they execute the stories and tasks they own. 

This is also incorporated in the rhythms of their strategic planning.

• Sprint – 3-week sprints completing stories and tasks, which contribute to:

• Plan – 3-sprint plans for the stories and features being produced over 9 weeks, which contribute to:

• Season – 6-month strategy for new features, which contribute to:

• Vision – 18-month plan for epics, revised every 6 months.  

The line of autonomy is addressed with “feature chats” where each team has 15 minutes to present their 3-Sprint Plan to management, discussing features, technical debt, and issues or dependencies. 

Microsoft’s Developer Division alone contained over 4,000 people working in hundreds of teams of 10-12 people, working in 3-week sprints. This is what they mean by “Agile at Scale”. More information can be found in Microsoft’s [DevOps Resource Centre](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/learn/agile/what-is-agile).
